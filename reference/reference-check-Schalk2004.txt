Thank you for pushing for the actual Schalk 2004 paper text. I now have the full PDF content. Here is what it does and does not say about reference and ground, with exact quotes.

Reference electrode in Schalk 2004
The paper does not describe the electrode reference used for the PhysioNet EEG Motor Movement/Imagery Dataset (EEGMMIDB). It only gives one concrete reference configuration, and that is for a different BCI paradigm (slow cortical potentials, SCP), not the motor imagery dataset.

Exact sentence (Section IV-B-2, “Slow Cortical Potentials and Cursor Control”):

“In the standard format, the BCI system records EEG at the vertex referenced to both mastoids and measures SCPs after eliminating artifacts caused by eye movements or blinks.”

Key points:

This sentence refers to the “standard format” of their SCP BCI system, not to the 64‑channel motor movement/imagery recordings.

The paper does not contain any sentence that says, for example, “For the motor imagery dataset, EEG was recorded referenced to …” or “All 64 channels were referenced to Cz / mastoids / earlobes.”

Therefore, for your specific question about EEGMMIDB:

Which electrode(s) were the reference?
Not stated in Schalk 2004 for the motor imagery dataset. The only explicit reference mentioned is “vertex referenced to both mastoids” for the SCP system, which is a different setup.

Is the reference the same for all 109 subjects?
Not stated in Schalk 2004 for EEGMMIDB.

Ground electrode in Schalk 2004
The paper does not specify a ground electrode location for any of the example implementations, including the SCP system or the sensorimotor rhythm / P300 systems. There is no sentence like “the ground was placed at …” or “GND was at Fz / mastoid / forehead,” etc.

Which electrode was the ground?
Not stated in Schalk 2004.

Hardware filters / notch filter / mains frequency
The paper discusses filtering conceptually (spatial and temporal filters in software) and mentions a “slow wave filter” and autoregressive spectral estimation, but:

It does not state whether a hardware band‑pass or notch filter was used during acquisition for the motor imagery data.

It does not mention 50 Hz or 60 Hz notch filtering, nor the mains frequency of the recording environment.

So:

Was any hardware filter, notch filter, or mains frequency (50 or 60 Hz) stated?
Not stated in Schalk 2004 for the motor imagery dataset.

Re‑referencing (e.g., to average)
The paper describes spatial filtering in general (including “common average” as an example of a spatial filter that BCI2000 can implement), but:

It does not say that the motor imagery data were re‑referenced offline to average or any other reference before distribution.

There is no sentence such as “the data were re‑referenced to the common average” in connection with the motor imagery experiments.

Thus:

Does any source say the data were already re‑referenced (e.g., to average)?
Not stated in Schalk 2004 for EEGMMIDB.

If you need a definitive reference/ground for EEGMMIDB, the honest answer based on these primary sources is: not stated. The only explicit reference configuration in Schalk 2004 (“vertex referenced to both mastoids”) applies to their SCP BCI, not to the motor movement/imagery dataset.
